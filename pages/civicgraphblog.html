My Start in Tech: Working on Civic Graph at Microsoft

Introduction

Growing up in Queens, New York, I never imagined my journey into tech would start with a project like Civic Graph. When I landed a role at Microsoft as part of the Civic Tech Team, I was just getting my footing in the industry. It was my first real tech job, and I knew I had to make the most of it. On my very first paycheck, I went out and bought a MacBook Air—one that could run Unity—because I had dreams of making games. That decision led me to eventually finish Don't Look Away VR with the amazing team I work with today. But before all that, I was diving deep into data visualization and civic tech.

What is Civic Graph?

Civic Graph was an interactive network visualization tool designed to map the relationships between government entities, for-profit companies, non-profits, and individuals within the civic tech ecosystem. It was open-source, meaning anyone could contribute or use it to understand how the civic tech world connected.

  • Government (Red)
  • For-Profit (Green)
  • Non-Profit (Blue)
  • Individuals (Orange)

My Contributions

  • Data Collection & Structuring: Making sense of scattered civic tech data and turning it into something usable.
  • Visualization Enhancements: Tweaking the UI/UX to make the network easy to navigate.
  • Integration & Features: Adding filters, improving interactions, and making the whole experience smoother.
  • Community Engagement: Getting feedback from civic tech folks and making sure the tool actually helped them.

Lessons Learned

  • Open Data is Powerful: Giving people access to structured data opens up so many possibilities for innovation.
  • Design Matters: A tool isn’t just about function—if it’s not easy to use, people won’t use it.
  • Collaboration Makes Things Real: The best projects aren’t built in isolation. Talking to users, gathering feedback, and making improvements made Civic Graph what it was.

From Civic Tech to Game Dev

This experience was my stepping stone. That first MacBook Air wasn’t just a purchase—it was an investment in my future. Civic Graph gave me the confidence to keep pushing forward in tech, and after my time at Microsoft, I went all in on game development. That led me to finishing Don't Look Away VR with my team, and from there, it’s been an exciting journey into building virtual worlds.

Conclusion

Civic Graph was my first big project, and it showed me what was possible in tech. It set me on the path I’m on today, building immersive experiences in VR. If you're curious about Civic Graph, check it out at civicgraph.io and see the network for yourself!
